# Reference data — not page content

## kbp_store_master.json

262 KFC restaurants from
`2015 KFC ASAP Pilot Restaurants Kulp - ALL KBP Surveyed Stores.xlsx`,
attached to a June 2017 email from Tony Miceli of KBP Foods.

### What it is for

Resolving store numbers. The crew wrote `KFC(356)` in photo-email subjects;
this file says that is `G135356`, 8939 W 7 Mile Rd, Detroit MI. All nine stores
named in the photo emails resolve against it, and it supplies a street number
for one the email left incomplete.

### What it is NOT for

**Publishing.** This is a client's own list of their restaurant locations — the
whole estate they asked to be surveyed and bid, most of it work this company
never did. Putting 262 KBP addresses on a public website would publish a
customer's confidential property schedule, and would also imply a body of work
the documents do not support: 246 of the 262 are marked "Not Started".

Only stores with independent completion evidence — a punch list, an
after-photograph, an invoice — may appear on a page. See
`src/data/georgiaStores.js` and `src/data/carolinaProgram.js` for the grading.

## kickserv_customers — the register, and where it lives

**The register itself is NOT in this repository.** `scripts/preserve_kickserv_customers.py`
writes it to `private/`, which is gitignored, and commits only
`kickserv_customers.summary.json` — counts, no people.

That is not squeamishness. 1,785 of these records are private individuals at
their home addresses with their phone numbers and email addresses beside them.
A git remote is a durable, replicated, widely-cloned place, and it is the wrong
place for that. Preservation means the operator holds the file; it does not mean
every clone of every repo holds it.

**So the register only survives if it is kept somewhere.** Regenerate it from
the export archive at any time with:

```
python scripts/preserve_kickserv_customers.py <kickserv-export.zip>
```

Keep both the export archive and the generated register in the operator's own
storage. Losing both loses the customer base.

The customer base, 2,263 records, preserved out of the Kickserv export
`jwordenandsonspaving_20260823215614.zip`. Generated by
`scripts/preserve_kickserv_customers.py`; the reader is
`app/services/kickserv_customers.py`.

### Why it is checked in

Kickserv is the system this business ran on from March 2013. When that account
lapses, the customer base goes with it — 2,263 customers, 683 named contacts,
eleven years of who called and what was done. Nothing else in this repository
holds them.

A database is not preservation. A database gets rebuilt, restored from a stale
backup, or lost. This file is the durable artefact and the CRM is loaded *from*
it (`app/services/kickserv_customer_load.py`), not the other way round.

### What is in it

| | |
|---|---|
| Customers | 2,263 — 478 commercial, 1,785 residential |
| With an email | 1,852 |
| With a phone | 2,038 |
| With a service address | 2,137 |
| Already geocoded by Kickserv | 804 |
| With at least one completed job | 920 |
| Franchise-branded (KFC, Taco Bell…) | 338 |
| Home market | VA 1,722, then GA 72, MI 50, FL 39, NC 34, TX 30 |

Each record carries the customer's own contacts, their job counts, and
`completed_revenue_cents` — which counts **only** jobs Kickserv marked
completed. That is a floor and is labelled one, for the same reason
`kickserv_import.py` refuses to publish the $41.3M charge-line sum: 1,333 jobs
in this archive carry no completion date at all.

`sms_unreachable` is **not** a marketing opt-out. All 177 values are delivery
failures — "Invalid number", "Landline or unreachable carrier". Reading them as
consent withdrawals would wrongly mark 177 contactable customers do-not-call.

### What it is NOT for

**Publishing.** 1,785 of these are private individuals at their home addresses,
with their phone numbers and email addresses beside them. None of it may reach a
page. The rule already applied throughout this repository stands: a residential
customer contributes a town and nothing else, and a commercial client's business
name may be named only where the work itself is evidenced.

`tests/backend/test_kickserv_customers.py` enforces it against the actual build
output rather than trusting the convention. Those tests skip when `private/` is
absent — on a fresh clone there are no people to check, and a skip is honest
where a silent pass over an empty directory is not.

## drive_media_index.json

An index of the media in Google Drive (`genewgeorge@gmail.com`), 52 files, built
2026-08-25.

### Why an index and not the files

Two hard limits, and neither is a matter of preference:

**The Takeout archive is unreachable.** 28 parts at ~2.1 GB each, ~59 GB total,
requested 2026-05-03. A Drive download returns base64-encoded content, so a 2 GB
zip arrives as roughly 2.7 GB of text. That is three orders of magnitude past
what can be handled, and no amount of chunking changes it.

**The individual files are not much better in bulk.** A 3 MB photograph is ~4 MB
of base64. A handful is feasible; a hundred is not. So the index records what
exists, where, and how much it can be trusted — and the files themselves get
uploaded by hand, in the small number that are actually wanted.

### The grades, and the one that matters

| grade | what it means |
|---|---|
| `owner_named` | The operator named the file himself and the name states a place or a job — `SELA PARKING LOT HOPEWELL`, `DRIVEWAY SEAL COAT OFF WESTHAM PARKWAY`. His own label on his own photograph. Same standing as any service-area claim on the site. |
| `dated_capture` | A camera filename carrying a capture date. The date is real; what the photograph SHOWS is unverified until a person looks at it. |
| `seo_named` | Named for a search term — `asphalt driveway richmond va.jpg`. May be a real job, may be stock. **Not publishable as our work without confirmation.** |
| `marketing_set` | Generated or sourced for a specific site build (the Minnesota set). Not evidence of a job. |

Only `owner_named` is publishable, and 18 files qualify. They name Richmond,
Chesterfield and Hopewell, and cover chip-and-tar, sealcoating, brick pavers and
gravel — services the site claims and has had no picture for.

`seo_named` is called out separately rather than lumped in with the rest on
purpose. A file called `cvs asphalt paving.jpg` looks like proof of a CVS job
and may be a stock photograph someone renamed for a page. Publishing it as our
work would be the fabricated store database again, in image form.

### The nine that matter most

Nine files carry capture dates of **2023-11-30** and **2023-12-01** — inside the
gap where the Kickserv job book holds nothing at all (its last record is
2022-04-04). They are the only recent evidence found anywhere so far. Whether
they show paving work is unverified; if they do, they are worth more than
anything else in this index, because recency of first-hand experience is exactly
what the ranking systems weigh and the record currently makes this company look
dormant since 2022.

What Sealcoating Actually Does

A sealcoat is a thin, flexible protective layer applied over sound asphalt. It is not paint and it is not a structural repair — it's a barrier that defends the surface from the four things that destroy Minnesota pavement:

💧 Locks Out Water

Water is the root cause of nearly all pavement failure here. Sealcoat fills surface voids and stops water from reaching the base, where it freezes, expands, and tears asphalt apart.

❄️ Survives Freeze-Thaw

Polymer-modified emulsion stays flexible and moves with the pavement through dozens of freeze-thaw swings a year, instead of going brittle and cracking like coal tar.

☀️ Blocks UV & Oxidation

Sun bakes the binder out of unprotected asphalt, leaving it gray, dry, and raveling. Sealcoat shields against UV and restores a rich black finish.

🛢️ Resists Salt & Petroleum

De-icing salt, gas, and oil drips soften and pit bare asphalt. A sealed surface resists chemical attack and is far easier to clean.

Why Minnesota Pavement Needs Sealcoating More Than Most

Our climate is uniquely hard on asphalt. Frost depths reach 50 inches or more, and the ground heaves and settles through the season. Every thaw lets water into the surface; every freeze expands it and pries the pavement open a little further. Across a single winter, a driveway can see dozens of these cycles — each one widening cracks and loosening the surface.

Sealcoating is the most cost-effective defense in that environment. By keeping water out of the surface in the first place, it interrupts the cycle that does the damage. That's why a sealed, maintained driveway routinely outlasts an unsealed neighbor's by a decade or more — at a tiny fraction of the cost of replacement.

Our Sealcoating Process

Premium results come from prep, not just product. Here's exactly how every job runs:

1. Clean & Prep

We blow, sweep, and power-clean the surface, kill any vegetation in cracks, and treat oil spots so the sealer bonds. A dirty surface is the number-one cause of early failure — we don't skip it.

2. Crack Fill & Repair

Cracks over 1/4" get hot-pour rubberized filler (ASTM D6690). Potholes and failed spots are patched first. Sealcoat protects a sound surface — it can't bridge open cracks.

3. Two-Coat Application

Polymer-modified emulsion with silica sand for traction, applied in two squeegee coats — the second perpendicular to the first for full, even coverage and a durable finish.

4. Cure & Inspect

We keep traffic off 24–48 hours, then inspect. Commercial lots get re-striping and ADA layout, and you get a simple re-seal schedule to protect the investment.

100% Coal-Tar-Free — Minnesota Compliant

Coal tar sealers are banned in Minneapolis and St. Paul and increasingly restricted statewide because of PAH runoff into lakes and groundwater. We use only polymer-modified asphalt emulsion: safer for your family and our waters, lower odor, and better suited to freeze-thaw. Read about Minnesota's coal tar ban →

Our Sealcoating Specifications

Material: Polymer-Modified Asphalt Emulsion

Commercial-grade AE-P emulsion with polymer cross-linking for enhanced flexibility and durability. Minimum 50% solids content. Silica sand aggregate at 3–5 lbs per gallon for skid resistance.

Application: Two-Coat System

First coat applied by squeegee for maximum penetration. Second coat applied perpendicular to the first for uniform coverage. Minimum 0.15 gallons per square yard per coat.

Crack Repair: Hot-Pour Rubberized

DOT-spec hot-pour rubberized crack filler (ASTM D6690 Type II) applied to all cracks > 1/4" before sealcoating. Prevents water bypass and freeze-thaw recurrence.

Striping & ADA Compliance

Full parking lot re-striping with traffic paint. ADA-compliant handicap stalls, access aisles, and signage. Fire lane marking and directional arrows included.

Sealcoating FAQ

How much does sealcoating cost in Minnesota?

Residential driveway sealcoating typically runs about $300–$600 for an average driveway, depending on size, condition, and crack filling needed. Commercial lots are priced per square foot on a sliding scale by area. Estimates are always free.

How often should I sealcoat?

Most Minnesota driveways should be sealcoated every 2–3 years, and commercial lots on a 3-year cycle. New asphalt should cure 6–12 months before its first coat. Over-sealing too often can cause buildup, so a measured rhythm is best.

When is the best time of year to sealcoat in Minnesota?

The season runs May through September. Application needs surface temperatures above 50°F, ideally daytime highs in the 60s–80s, with no rain in the 24-hour forecast. Book by July for best availability.

Do you use coal tar sealer?

No. We use polymer-modified asphalt emulsion only. Coal tar is banned in Minneapolis and St. Paul and increasingly restricted statewide due to PAH runoff. Emulsion is safer, lower-odor, and flexes with the freeze-thaw cycle instead of cracking.

How long does it take to dry?

Sealcoat is usually dry to the touch in 4–8 hours. We recommend keeping foot traffic off for 24 hours and vehicles off for 24–48 hours, depending on temperature and humidity.

Will sealcoating fix cracks and potholes?

No — sealcoating is a protective coating, not a structural repair. We crack-fill and patch first, then sealcoat to protect that sound surface from water and UV. See our asphalt repair services.

Is sealcoating really worth it here?

Yes — arguably more than almost anywhere. Minnesota's deep frost and frequent freeze-thaw cycles drive water-related pavement failure, and sealcoating locks water out. A sealed surface routinely lasts far longer than an unsealed one for a small fraction of replacement cost.

Do you handle both driveways and commercial lots?

Yes. We cover single driveways, long lake-cabin drives, retail centers, HOA communities, schools, and municipal lots, with multi-year maintenance programs available for commercial clients.
